Welcome to Manthan, organized by the Department of Computer Science and Engineering, Data Science. Your challenge is to build an autonomous trading system, a bot that analyzes market data, makes trading decisions, and executes trades automatically. In this video, we'll walk you through the platform, the development process, and everything you need to prepare your bot for the live simulation. You'll have approximately 20 to 22 hours to develop your trading system. After development, your bot will participate in a live simulation lasting approximately one hour. Based on your simulation performance, selected teams will be shortlisted. The judges will then evaluate the shortlisted teams and select the winner. So focus on building a reliable system, making sound trading decisions and managing your portfolio effectively. Let's understand how the platform works. The platform streams real-time market ticks and company news through Kafka. Your bot consumes this information and applies your chosen trading strategy to decide which stock to trade, what action to take, and when to do nothing. When your bot decides to place a trade, it sends an order request to the order API. The API executes the order at the latest traded price and updates your portfolio. That's the basic flow. Consume market data, make a decision, submit an order, and track the results. Before you begin, open the competition portal and locate your team's configuration details. You'll find the Kafka broker address, consumer group, and bearer token required to connect to the platform and access the APIs. Every API request must include your bearer token in the Authorization header. Keep your credentials secure. Never expose your actual tokens or secrets in screenshots, public repositories, or your video demonstrations. During the pre-flight phase, prepare your bot to discover the available stocks dynamically. Use the GetStocks API to retrieve the list of tradable ticker symbols. Build or update your bot's ticker list using the API response rather than hard-coding stock symbols. This ensures your system can adapt to the stocks made available by the platform. Before the simulation starts, make sure your bot can process pre-flight events correctly. During this phase, the platform publishes events named pre-flight ticks on the Kafka topic market.ticks. Each event contains a sequence number. Your bot must acknowledge these events by sending a POST request to the bot heartbeat endpoint, including the corresponding sequence number. The endpoint is shown on-screen. Implement and test this flow during pre-flight. Make sure your bot is processing events and sending the required acknowledgments before the simulation begins. Your bot receives market data through the market.ticks Kafka topic. Each tick contains important information about a stock, including its opening price, highest price, lowest price, closing price, and trading volume. These are commonly known as OHLCV values. The event also includes a timestamp and the simulated market date. Use these values to track price movements, calculate indicators, identify patterns, and make informed trading decisions. In addition to market ticks, the platform provides company news through the market.news Kafka topic. News events contain a headline and description that your bot can use to understand potential market-moving developments. Using news is optional. You can build a strategy based entirely on market data, incorporate news sentiment, or combine both approaches. Possible strategies include moving averages, the Relative Strength Index (RSI), mean reversion, momentum trading, statistical arbitrage, sentiment analysis, or language model-based news analysis. These are examples, not restrictions. You're free to choose the approach that works best for your team. Beyond the mandatory requirements, the possibilities are yours to explore. Want to build a custom frontend to monitor your bot, visualize market data, and manage your system? Go for it. Want to tweak your strategy, adjust parameters or switch approaches during the simulation? Build the capability to do so. You can combine different technologies, experiment with new ideas, and build anything you believe gives your team an edge. There's just one critical constraint. This is a real-time simulation. Your trading decisions should take no more than 3 to 4 seconds. A brilliant decision that arrives too late can mean a missed opportunity. So be creative, experiment boldly, and build beyond the basics. The platform provides the foundation. What you build on top of it is up to you. You don't have to build everything from scratch. You're welcome to use tools such as ChatGPT, GitHub Copilot, Cursor, and pre-trained machine learning models. Use them to accelerate development, explore ideas, write code, analyze data, and improve your system. What matters is the trading system you build and how effectively it performs during the simulation. When the portal indicates that the live simulation has started, your bot can begin placing orders. To submit a trade, send a POST request to the Orders API. The endpoint is shown on-screen. Your request must include the stock ticker, the trading action, and the positive whole-number quantity. The platform executes the order at the latest traded price and updates your portfolio accordingly. Remember, wait until the portal shows live simulation before placing orders. Your bot can perform four types of trading actions. Buy means purchasing shares. Sell means selling shares you already hold. Short means opening a short position to potentially benefit from a price decline. Cover means closing an existing short position. Choose the appropriate action based on your strategy, current positions, available cash and risk management rules. Your bot should also recognise when the best decision is to do nothing. Each team starts with virtual capital of 10 lakh rupees. Use the participant dashboard to monitor your portfolio throughout the simulation. Total Wealth shows the overall value of your portfolio. Available Cash shows your uncommitted cash. Invested Value represents the value of your holdings, while Locked Margin reflects the margin committed to positions. You can also monitor trading costs and net profit and loss. Under active positions, you can view the quantity held, latest traded price, position valuation, and open profit or loss. Monitor these metrics regularly and ensure your strategy accounts for both potential returns and trading costs. The competition leaderboard is available to administrators, coordinators and judges. Your focus as a participant should be on building a reliable trading bot, responding to market changes, and managing your portfolio effectively. You have the platform, the tools, and the freedom to experiment. Now it's time to turn your ideas into a working autonomous trading system. Build smart, test thoroughly, trade responsibly.
